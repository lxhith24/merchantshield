00

The whole system on one page

Five layers. An application enters at the top and can only leave through a person.

MerchantShield answers one narrow question: are these merchant applications better explained by a coordinated ring, or by a legitimate shared arrangement — an accountant filing for clients, a franchise, a coworking floor, a family business, a shared kiosk?

The answer is never a verdict. It is a case: a scored candidate group, a set of auditable evidence edges, a written explanation whose every citation was mechanically checked, and a queue position in front of a named reviewer.

01

Ingress

Three entry points, one internal representation, no privileged path.

The review desk runs against a frozen population of 104 synthetic applications, generated from a fixed seed and split by whole connected component so a candidate a reviewer opens was never in the training partition of the model scoring it.

The live merchant check accepts pasted or uploaded CSV rows and runs them through the same evidence graph, the same router and the same investigator. It is deliberately amnesiac: nothing is fitted, persisted, approved, rejected, or sent to a gateway, and the batch is bounded at 1 MB and 100 rows. Its purpose is to show the real machinery on new input, not to become a second, weaker decision path.

The seven rehearsed scenarios include two that exist to fail on purpose — a shared-kiosk false positive that a reviewer must clear, and a fabricated-citation case that the grounding validator must catch.

02

The evidence layer

Raw identifiers become hashed, weighted, individually addressable edges — and then stop.

Each application in a ring looks fine alone. The ring becomes visible only through what its members involuntarily share: the settlement account the money lands in, the device the forms were typed on, the address block, the IP, the phone batch.

Every shared value is hashed before it is used, so the graph, the models, the tools and the investigator all reason over a9f2c1… rather than an account number. The hash is the join key and the privacy boundary — the same mechanism does both jobs, which is why it cannot be forgotten.

Attribute severities, and why they are not equal

A shared settlement account is close to conclusive: it is where the money actually ends up. A shared IP could be a coworking floor, a café, or carrier-grade NAT. The weights encode that asymmetry directly rather than burying it in a fitted parameter.

Two further guards keep weak-but-popular signals from dominating. An attribute value shared by more than 50 applications is dropped entirely — that is infrastructure, not a ring. And a value shared by more than two applications has its severity divided by a frequency discount, gently for strong attributes and by √(n−1) for weak ones, so a public email domain connecting nine merchants does not become evidence merely by connecting nine merchants.

03

The routed sparse mixture of experts

A system-level MoE: ordinary components behind one contract, with a deterministic gate deciding who runs.

This is not a trained neural MoE and it is not several chatbots debating. Three ordinary experts implement one evaluate() contract; a deterministic router decides which of them runs and what their disagreement means.

The rules expert and the graph expert always run, concurrently, each behind a 2-second timeout and a per-expert exception boundary. A crashed or slow expert returns an explicit FAILED result with an error code, which becomes a reason to review — never a silent zero. Missing evidence is never clean evidence is enforced here, in the failure path, not in a comment.

The tabular expert is the sparse part: it runs only under three named conditions.

What the router may and may not conclude

The policy is a fold over reason codes, not a model. Any code at all produces human_review; an empty list produces proceed_to_onboarding. There is no third outcome — no autonomous rejection exists anywhere in the mounted application.

The graph expert's number is the risk score, full stop. The rules and tabular experts can add reason codes and therefore cause a review, but neither can lower graph risk, and neither replaces the number a reviewer sees.

Honesty about the sparsity claim: on the current benchmark 94.8% of applications still invoke the specialist, so routing saves very little compute today. It is a correctness and auditability mechanism first — every invocation carries the reason it happened — and an efficiency mechanism only in principle.

04

The workflow state machine

Four LangGraph nodes, one interrupt, and a checkpoint that is safe to store.

The graph's contract is narrow on purpose. assess is a thin wrapper over the routed MoE — none of its rules, scoring or policy are reimplemented here. investigate runs only when the assessment already demanded review. finalize re-applies the deterministic policy over both results and is the only node permitted to name an action.

When the investigator needs a fact nobody has, the graph calls interrupt() and the case is checkpointed mid-flight. Supplying that evidence resumes the same case: the case id is a stable hash of the candidate id, and resume asserts it. A paused case never restarts and never loses its trace.

05

The bounded investigator and its grounding authority

Every path out of the model is narrower than the path in.

One LLM runs in the active workflow. Its job is narrow: given evidence deterministic experts already produced, is a coordinated ring or a legitimate shared arrangement the better explanation? It must state a ring hypothesis and at least one specific legitimate alternative — an accountant, a franchise, a coworking floor, family ownership, a shared kiosk — before it may conclude anything.

It selects from six read-only tools, each scope-checked to exactly one candidate, and is told to pick the next call from what it still does not know rather than calling everything. Its budget is hard: five reasoning steps, six tool calls, fifteen seconds, 2,048 output tokens. Exhausting any of them ends the investigation with an explicit failure code and sends the case to a person.

Why the validator is code and not a second model

The brief specification permitted a second, evidence-grounding LLM. It is not used. Citation checking is a set-membership question with an exact answer, and answering it in plain code makes it cheap, deterministic, testable and incapable of being talked out of a rejection. A second model would have added a failure mode without adding an ability.

The catalogue it checks against is built from the union of the candidate's graph edges and every evidence item each expert emitted — so it is precisely the set of things that genuinely belong to this case, and nothing else.

Prior-case memory

The investigator can read how humans resolved earlier cases on the same hashed infrastructure: confirmed ring, legitimate accountant, legitimate franchise, legitimate coworking, legitimate family, or inconclusive. This is an exact lookup over recorded outcomes, not vector retrieval and not free-form model recall. Prior outcomes may inform an explanation; they may never overwrite the graph expert's number.

06

Custody

Persistence, the human decision, and the one door to the outside world.

Every state change reads the current row, checks the caller's expected_version, writes the new values and its audit event in one transaction, and bumps the version. A stale version is rejected outright and returns the current one, never silently merged — so two reviewers on the same case get a visible conflict rather than a lost write.

The audit log is append-only and enforced at the model layer: case_opened, automated_decision_recorded, investigation_recorded, information_requested, information_supplied, case_claimed, case_released, human_decision_recorded, onboarding_handoff_recorded.

A reviewer's resolution is stored beside the automated decision, not over it. Reconstructing what the machine said before a person disagreed with it is always possible, which is the only version of accountability that survives contact with a real incident review.

The gateway is the single egress. A hand-off attempted without a recorded approve_onboarding raises HandoffNotPermitted. The default gateway is simulated; the Razorpay adapter refuses to run unless test-mode partner credentials are explicitly configured and the key carries the test prefix. There is no production mode and no fallback — an unconfigured adapter raises rather than degrading quietly.

Deliberately absent

The original automatic-decision API still exists in the repository but is not mounted on the reviewer application, because it can emit automatic rejection outcomes. It is retained only as an isolated backwards-compatibility fixture on a separate FastAPI instance. Nothing a reviewer touches can reach it.

Missing evidence is never clean evidence

Absent device or IP observations force review even below the risk threshold. An expert that times out produces a reason code, not a zero. A tool that cannot answer returns an explicit unavailable result rather than an empty one. A wider-context component above the 50-merchant cap requires human review regardless of score. Each of these is a place where the cheap failure mode would have been to treat silence as safety.

08

The evaluation plane

Structurally separate from the review desk, and read-only by construction.

Evaluation runs on its own datasets, against models fitted afresh in memory, and cannot reach a case. The challenger endpoint scores an existing candidate in shadow and is explicitly incapable of modifying a case, recording verification, approving or rejecting a merchant, or calling a gateway.

What the numbers actually say

On the 600-application held-out expanded benchmark, the shipped routed system reaches precision 0.393 at recall 0.991, with a false-positive rate of 0.930 and a 99.2% review rate. Ring recall is 1.000 — meaning at least one member of every labelled ring was flagged, not that rings were recovered whole.

That is a system that catches nearly everything by reviewing nearly everything. It is not production-ready, and the benchmark exists to make that visible rather than to flatter the design.

A sealed challenger model reached 0.612 precision and 0.835 PR-AUC on its own fresh test, cutting false flags from 486 to 160 — but merchant recall fell to 82.4% and ring recall to 87.8%, missing the 90% development target. It was not promoted. The review desk keeps its frozen model and cases; the challenger is available for read-only shadow scoring only. A separate wider-context experiment failed its adoption checks outright and is preserved as a negative result.

Every figure here is synthetic. None of it is Razorpay production performance.

09

Deployment

Two processes, one file, no secrets required.

The Streamlit reviewer UI on :8501 talks to the FastAPI service on :8000 over plain HTTP; it holds no business logic and no direct database handle. The API assembles one process-wide runtime on first use — fitting the graph and tabular models on the training partition, building the evidence graph, and wiring the workflow, store, service and gateway — behind an async lock.

State lives in a single SQLite file. Without ANTHROPIC_API_KEY the system runs in LLM_DISABLED mode: a deterministic investigator with the same tool-selection discipline stands in, and every surface displays the mode banner alongside the SIMULATED data and gateway labels. The full demonstration, all tests and the acceptance run need no key.

The reviewer UI always shows three labels — data, gateway, investigator — so nothing on screen can be mistaken for a production decision on real merchants.

Known limits

  • Sparse routing currently saves almost no compute: 94.8% of applications still invoke the specialist.
  • False-positive rates at the shipped threshold are high enough that the system's honest output is a review queue, not a filter.
  • Some slow legitimate shared-infrastructure groups and some slow labelled rings have genuinely overlapping available evidence. Separating them needs independent verified relationship evidence — settlement authority, ownership, authorised intermediaries, tenancy — not a more confident model.
  • No real identity verification, no document OCR in the active path, no production calibration, and designed rather than observed fraud prevalence.
  • Benchmark partitions share synthetic scenario families, so held-out generalisation is bounded by the generator's imagination.
